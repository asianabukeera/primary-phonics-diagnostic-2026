Executive Summary

The Primary Phonics Diagnostic Assessment was conducted to assess learner reading performance across Kindergarten, Primary 1, Primary 2, Primary 3 and Primary 4.

The assessment examined learner progression through five stages of reading: sound recognition, word reading, paragraph reading, story reading and reading comprehension.

The findings reveal that learner performance declines as reading tasks become increasingly complex. While many learners can recognise sounds, fewer successfully transition to fluent reading and comprehension.

Assessment Purpose

The assessment sought to determine:

  • Ability to recognise sounds.
  • Ability to decode words.
  • Ability to read connected text.
  • Reading fluency and accuracy.
  • Reading comprehension.

Key Finding 1: Foundational Sound Knowledge Remains Weak

Many Kindergarten and lower-primary learners experience difficulty producing correct sounds. A common pattern observed was that learners recognised letter names but struggled to produce corresponding letter sounds.

This indicates the need for stronger phonemic awareness and phonics instruction in early grade classrooms.

Key Finding 2: Word Blending Remains a Major Challenge

Many learners progressed beyond sound recognition but struggled with blending sounds into words. This limited progression to fluent reading.

The results suggest that decoding skills require further strengthening.

Key Finding 3: Paragraph Reading Is a Significant Bottleneck

Many learners in Primary 1 and Primary 2 demonstrated the ability to read individual words but experienced difficulties reading connected text.

This indicates gaps in reading fluency, automaticity and confidence.

Key Finding 4: Consonant Blends Challenge Upper Primary Learners

Learners in Primary 3 and Primary 4 commonly struggle with consonant blends and complex phonics patterns including:

  • ch
  • sh
  • th
  • bl
  • br
  • gr
  • str
  • spl
  • thr

These patterns remain a major barrier to fluent reading.

Key Finding 5: Comprehension Is Weaker Than Decoding

Several learners successfully decoded stories but struggled to answer comprehension questions. This demonstrates a gap between reading words accurately and understanding meaning.

Reading instruction should therefore strengthen comprehension alongside decoding and fluency.

Recommendations

  1. Strengthen phonemic awareness instruction in Kindergarten and Primary 1.
  2. Increase opportunities for guided blending and decoding practice.
  3. Provide targeted support for struggling readers.
  4. Strengthen instruction on consonant blends and advanced phonics patterns.
  5. Integrate comprehension activities into all reading lessons.
  6. Conduct regular phonics assessment and progress monitoring.
  7. Use assessment findings to guide remedial support and learner interventions.
